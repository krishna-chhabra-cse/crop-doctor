title: Tomato yellow leaf curl virus
---
## Symptoms
Leaves curl and cup, turn yellow at the edges, and stay small and crinkled. Plants become stunted and bushy with short gaps between leaves, and older leaves can become leathery and brittle. Flowers drop and fruit set falls sharply. Small white insects (whiteflies) are often seen under the leaves.

## Why it spreads
Caused by whitefly-transmitted viruses (tomato yellow leaf curl virus and the related tomato leaf curl viruses common in India). The whitefly Bemisia tabaci carries the virus from plant to plant; infected seedlings moved between farms also spread it. There is no cure for an infected plant.

## What to do now
Within 2-3 days, pull out and destroy infected plants, especially young ones, so whiteflies cannot pick up the virus from them. Control whiteflies: put up yellow sticky traps (TNAU suggests about 5 per acre) and, if whiteflies are many, use an insecticide labelled for whitefly. Remove weed hosts such as Abutilon indicum around the field.

## Organic and low-cost options
Yellow sticky traps, removing infected plants early, removing weed hosts, and growing tall barrier crops such as sorghum, maize or bajra around the field (TNAU recommendation) all reduce whitefly spread without chemicals.

## Chemical options
Sprays control the whitefly, not the virus. TNAU lists insecticides such as imidacloprid 17.8 SL (200 ml per acre), thiamethoxam 25 WG (200 g per acre), dimethoate 30 EC or methyl demeton 25 EC (200 ml per acre). Rotate insecticide groups because whiteflies quickly become resistant, and follow the label.

## Prevention next season
Raise seedlings under insect-proof net (protected nursery). Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available. Use disease-free seedlings, plant barrier crops, keep the field free of weed hosts, and avoid planting next to an old infected crop.
