## Symptoms
Pale green to yellow patches on the upper side of older leaves. Underneath those patches there is an olive-green, grey or brown velvety mould. Leaves later turn brown and wither. It is most common in greenhouses, polyhouses and high tunnels.

## Why it spreads
Caused by the fungus Passalora fulva (also called Fulvia fulva or Cladosporium fulvum). Spores germinate when the air is very humid (above about 85% relative humidity) and spread in moving air and on hands, clothes and tools.

## What to do now
Lower the humidity: open side walls, doors and vents and use fans, especially when humidity goes above about 85%. Water at the base, not over the leaves. Remove the worst-affected leaves. If you spray, cover the undersides of the leaves, where the mould grows.

## Organic and low-cost options
Ventilation, wider spacing, pruning lower leaves for air flow, and keeping greenhouse night temperatures a little above outside temperatures (to stop dew forming on leaves) are the cheapest and most effective steps. A copper hydroxide product gave some control in trials; it must be labelled for your crop and for greenhouse use.

## Chemical options
Use only fungicides labelled for tomato leaf mould and for greenhouse use, sprayed onto the undersides of leaves, and follow the label.

## Prevention next season
Choose resistant varieties (but the fungus has many races, so resistance may not cover all of them). Remove all crop debris after the season and clean greenhouse walls and benches. Keep humidity low from the start.
