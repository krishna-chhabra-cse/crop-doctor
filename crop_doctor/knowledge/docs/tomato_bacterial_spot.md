title: Tomato bacterial spot
---
## Symptoms
Many small (a few mm), dark brown to black spots that look greasy or water-soaked, sometimes with a yellow edge. Spots can join together and leaves turn yellow and drop. Fruit gets small raised, scabby spots.

## Why it spreads
Caused by Xanthomonas bacteria. It comes in on infected seed and transplants, and spreads by rain splash, overhead watering, and by hands and tools when leaves are wet. Warm, wet weather favours it. It is hard to control once it is established.

## What to do now
Do not work among the plants while leaves are wet. Remove badly infected plants or leaves. Switch to drip or ground-level watering. Disinfect tools. Start a copper-based spray programme to protect new growth.

## Organic and low-cost options
Fixed-copper products are the main option for bacterial spot and are allowed in organic farming. Many strains are now partly resistant to copper, so copper alone may not give good control; prevention matters most.

## Chemical options
Fixed copper is still the main field treatment; Alabama Extension advises tank-mixing copper with mancozeb to increase its effect. A plant-defence activator (acibenzolar-S-methyl, sold as Actigard) used with copper reduced disease in trials. Fungicides that only kill fungi (most others) do not work on bacteria. Follow the label.

## Prevention next season
Use certified disease-free seed and healthy transplants; this is the most important step. Rotate away from tomato and pepper for at least a year, destroy crop debris, and avoid overhead watering.
