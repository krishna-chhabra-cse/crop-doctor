title: Tomato Septoria leaf spot
---
## Symptoms
Many small round spots (about 3-6 mm) with dark brown edges and grey or pale centres. With a magnifying glass you can see tiny black dots inside the spots. It starts on the lowest leaves, usually after the first fruits set, and moves up; badly spotted leaves turn brown and dry up.

## Why it spreads
Caused by the fungus Septoria lycopersici. Spores are splashed by rain or overhead watering onto wet leaves. It spreads fast in warm, rainy weather and survives between crops on tomato debris and nightshade weeds.

## What to do now
Remove the spotted lower leaves and take them away from the field. Water at ground level (drip or a hose below the leaves), never over the leaves. Improve air flow by staking and spacing plants. Infected plants cannot be cured, but they often still give a good harvest, especially if infection comes late in the season.

## Organic and low-cost options
Copper-based fungicides, applied every 7-10 days, can help when the disease is severe. Good spacing, staking and ground-level watering are free and very effective.

## Chemical options
Products containing chlorothalonil, mancozeb, maneb or copper, applied every 7-10 days, can help when Septoria is severe and limits the harvest year after year. Always use them together with the cultural steps above and follow the product label.

## Prevention next season
Start with healthy, disease-free seedlings. Remove and destroy plant debris after the last harvest. Rotate away from tomato and other nightshade crops. Remove nightshade weeds. Keep leaves dry.
