title: Potato early blight
---
## Symptoms
Dark brown spots with rings inside them, like a target, usually on older, lower leaves first. Spots can be limited by leaf veins and look angular. Leaves may yellow around the spots and die. Tubers can get dark, slightly sunken spots.

## Why it spreads
Caused by the fungus Alternaria solani (the same fungus as tomato early blight). It survives on infected plant debris in the soil; wind and rain splash carry spores. Warm weather with alternating wet and dry periods, and stressed or older plants, favour it.

## What to do now
Remove badly spotted lower leaves. Avoid overhead watering. Keep plants well fed (nitrogen-starved plants get worse early blight). Early blight is best managed preventively, so if spots are spreading, start a fungicide programme and repeat as the label says.

## Organic and low-cost options
Copper-based fungicides can give some protection. Good plant nutrition, avoiding water stress and removing infected leaves help.

## Chemical options
Fungicides such as mancozeb, chlorothalonil, azoxystrobin or other labelled products protect healthy leaves; TNAU lists similar products for early blight on tomato (same fungus). Alternate products with different modes of action and follow the label and pre-harvest interval.

## Prevention next season
Rotate so potato or tomato is grown in the same field only every third or fourth year, giving infected debris time to break down. Use healthy seed tubers, destroy crop debris, and choose tolerant varieties if available.
