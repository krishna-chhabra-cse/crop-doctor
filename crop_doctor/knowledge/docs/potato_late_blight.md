title: Potato late blight
---
## Symptoms
Dark, water-soaked patches on leaves, often starting at the leaf tips or edges, that grow quickly and turn brown-black. In humid weather a white fuzzy growth appears at the edge of the patches, mostly under the leaf. Tubers can get reddish-brown, dry rot under the skin.

## Why it spreads
Caused by the water mould Phytophthora infestans, the same organism that causes tomato late blight. It survives in infected seed tubers and discarded (cull) potatoes, and its spores travel on the wind. Cool, wet, humid weather makes it spread through a field within days.

## What to do now
Act within 24 hours. Remove and destroy infected plants (bag or bury them; do not compost). Spray the rest of the crop with a protectant fungicide straight away and repeat as the label says. Tell neighbours growing potato or tomato.

## Organic and low-cost options
Copper-based fungicides are the main organic option; they protect but do not cure. Remove infected plants quickly and destroy piles of cull potatoes, which are a major source of spores.

## Chemical options
Protectant fungicides (such as mancozeb or chlorothalonil) work against all known strains if applied before infection; some strains resist metalaxyl. Fungicides alone will not control late blight: combine them with the cultural steps. Follow the label, alternate modes of action, and respect the pre-harvest interval.

## Prevention next season
Plant certified, healthy seed potatoes. Destroy cull piles and volunteer potato plants. Rotate fields. Hill the soil well over tubers so spores cannot reach them, and kill the vines before harvest if the leaves are infected.
