title: Tomato early blight
---
## Symptoms
Brown spots with rings inside them, like a target or bull's eye, often with a yellow edge. Spots appear first on older, lower leaves and move upwards. Fruit can get dark, sunken spots with rings near the stem end.

## Why it spreads
Caused by the fungus Alternaria. It survives in old crop debris and can come in on seed. Rain splash and wind carry spores to new leaves. Warm (about 24-29 °C), humid and rainy weather favours it.

## What to do now
Remove badly spotted lower leaves and take them out of the field (do not leave them on the ground). Stop overhead watering; water at the base of the plant. If many plants are affected, start a fungicide programme at the first signs and repeat every 7-10 days as the label says.

## Organic and low-cost options
Copper-based fungicides can give some control when started at the first sign of spots and repeated every 7-10 days. Treating seed with the biocontrol fungus Trichoderma asperellum (about 4 g per kg of seed) is recommended by TNAU before sowing.

## Chemical options
TNAU (Tamil Nadu Agricultural University) lists sprays such as mancozeb, metiram, zineb, azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin, or mixtures like azoxystrobin + difenoconazole, at the rates on the product label (for example azoxystrobin 23% SC at 200 ml per acre). Use only one product at a time, alternate products with different modes of action, and follow the label and the waiting period before harvest.

## Prevention next season
Rotate crops so tomato, potato and other nightshade crops are not grown in the same field every year (a gap of 2-3 years helps). Destroy crop debris after harvest. Remove nightshade weeds. Use healthy seed or seedlings and, where available, tolerant varieties. Stake and space plants so leaves dry quickly.
